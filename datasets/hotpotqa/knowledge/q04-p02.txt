# Djamaâ el Kebir

[0] The Great Mosque of Algiers (Arabic: الجامع الكبير‎ ‎ , "Jemaa Kebir") or “Djama’a al-Kebir” (meaning Great Mosque) is a mosque in Algiers, Algeria, located very close to Algiers Harbor.
[1]  An inscription on the minbar (منبر) or the pulpit testifies to fact that the mosque was built in 1097.
[2]  It is also known by several other names such as Grand Mosque d'Alger, Djamaa al-Kebir, El Kebir Mosque and Jami Masjid.
[3]  It is one of the few remaining examples of Almoravid architecture.
[4]  It is the oldest mosque in Algiers and is said to be the oldest mosque in Algeria after Sidi Okba Mosque.
[5]  It was built under sultan Ali ibn Yusuf.
[6]  Its minaret dates from 1332 (1324 in some sources) and was built by the Ziyyanid Sultan of Tlemcen.
[7]  The gallery at the outside of the mosque was built in 1840.
[8]  Its construction was a consequence of a complete reconstruction of the street by the French.
